DID YOU KNOW · TRADITION ⇄ SCIENCE

One rhythm, many lenses

A rotating feed — how different traditions read the breath, and where science agrees.
SWARA YOGA · INDIA

The side you breathe from was a clock.

Tradition says

Ida (left) cools and calms; Pingala (right) heats and energizes. Practitioners timed tasks to the dominant nostril.

Science says

Airflow really does alternate between nostrils — the nasal cycle, driven by the autonomic nervous system. In ~72% of people, over hours (mean ~2.6 h), and more irregular than the tradition assumed.

Where they meet: tradition noticed the alternation centuries before rhinomanometry could measure it.
CHRONOBIOLOGY · MODERN

Your body runs ~90-minute cycles all day.

Science says

Kleitman's Basic Rest-Activity Cycle: alertness and rest oscillate through the day. Some work ties the nasal cycle to these ultradian swings and to alternating brain-hemisphere activity.

Tradition echoes

Yogic texts already mapped the day into active and restful windows — and told you to work with them, not against them.

Where they meet: both say attention isn't flat — it comes in waves worth noticing.
BREATH & NERVOUS SYSTEM

Slow breathing can settle you — measurably.

Science says

Slow, nasal, extended-exhale breathing raises heart-rate variability and vagal (parasympathetic) tone, and can lower stress and anxiety.

Tradition says

Pranayama has taught breath-pacing for calm and focus for millennia — the left-nostril / cooling practices especially.

Where they meet: the calming technique is old; the mechanism (vagal tone, HRV) is now measurable.

EVIDENCE · KNOWN VS. BELIEVED

Claims, graded honestly

Every statement in the app carries its evidence level. We don't confirm beliefs — we label them.
Established

The nasal cycle is real

Airflow dominance alternates between nostrils under autonomic control of the nasal turbinates. Seen in ~72% of people, typically over hours (mean ~2.6 h) and irregular. Documented since 1895, confirmed with rhinomanometry.

Kayser 1895 · Hasegawa & Kern 1977 · Eccles 1996 · Kahana-Zweig 2016
Established

Slow breathing calms the nervous system

Slow, paced breathing (<10 / min) raises heart-rate variability and vagal tone, shifting you toward “rest.” The app's strongest evidence — and it doesn't depend on which nostril.

Zaccaro et al. 2018 (review) · Russo et al. 2017 (review)
Emerging / contested

Nostril side ↔ autonomic state

Small studies link right-nostril breathing to arousal and left to calming — but samples are tiny and one controlled study found no effect. Real question, unsettled answer. That's why you test it.

Werntz 1983 · Telles 1994 · Pal 2014 · Larson 1993 (null)
Preliminary

Effects on focus & cognition

That dominance predicts your focus, energy or mood is not established — one small study saw a spatial-task effect, others didn't. Exactly what your own log can explore, n of 1.

Jella & Shannahoff-Khalsa 1993 · mixed findings
Traditional

The Swara interpretation

Swara yoga read this rhythm long before instruments could measure it — left cooling (Ida), right heating (Pingala), balance as stillness (Sushumna). A remarkable early observation; honored here as heritage and as the source of the suggestions you test.

Shiva Svarodaya (trad.)
