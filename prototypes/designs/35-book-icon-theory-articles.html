Theory
Established

The nasal cycle is real

Physiology · ~4 min read

Right now, one of your nostrils is probably breathing more freely than the other. A few hours from now, they'll likely have swapped. This isn't congestion — it's the nasal cycle: erectile tissue in each nostril slowly swells and shrinks, steered by your autonomic nervous system.

The German physician Richard Kayser first measured it in 1895. Modern studies confirm it — but with a caveat worth knowing: only about 72% of people show a clear cycle, its length is usually hours (averaging ~2.6 h), and it's irregular, not a tidy clock.

Why it matters here: the thing you're logging is real, measurable physiology — that's the solid ground the app stands on.
