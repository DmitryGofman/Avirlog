# AvirLog — Research backbone (verified sources)

The scientific reframe rests on one honest sentence:

> **We reliably measure which nostril is open. The nasal cycle is real
> physiology. The *meaning* of a given side — active vs. calming, Pingala vs.
> Ida — is a hypothesis from tradition with only preliminary and partly
> contradicted scientific support. The app helps you test it on yourself.**

Every citation below was checked against PubMed / publisher pages. Grades:
**Established** (well-replicated) · **Emerging** (some studies, mixed) ·
**Preliminary** (small/single studies) · **Traditional** (heritage text).

---

## 1 · The nasal cycle is real — Established

- **Kayser, R. (1895).** *Die exacte Messung der Luftdurchgängigkeit der Nase.*
  Arch. für Laryngologie und Rhinologie. First Western description of
  alternating nasal airflow. (Historical foundation; pre-modern, no PMID.)
- **Hasegawa, M. & Kern, E.B. (1977).** *The human nasal cycle.* Mayo Clin.
  Proc. 52(1):28–34. In 50 subjects over ~7 h: **72% showed a clearly defined
  cycle; mean duration ~2.6 h.** (Commonly cited as PMID 609284 — verify before
  printing the number.)
- **Eccles, R. (1996).** *A role for the nasal cycle in respiratory defence.*
  Eur. Respir. J. 9(2):371–376. **PMID 8777979.** Review; autonomic control of
  venous sinusoids; cycle intensifies during infection.
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8777979/>
- **Kahana-Zweig, R. et al. (2016).** *Measuring and Characterizing the Human
  Nasal Cycle.* PLOS ONE 11(10):e0162918. **PMID 27711189.** Long-duration
  home measurement — confirms the cycle but shows it is **more variable and
  individual** than the tidy "clockwork" popular accounts imply.
  <https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0162918>

> **In-app precision:** only ~72% show a *clearly defined* cycle; the period is
> **hours (mean ~2.6 h), not minutes**; it is irregular, not a reliable clock.
> Popular Swara sources overstate its regularity and shortness.

## 2 · Slow breathing changes physiology — Established (strongest hook)

- **Zaccaro, A. et al. (2018).** *How Breath-Control Can Change Your Life: A
  Systematic Review on Psycho-Physiological Correlates of Slow Breathing.*
  Front. Hum. Neurosci. 12:353. **PMC6137615 / PMID 30245619.** Slow breathing
  (<10 bpm) raises HRV, RSA, and alpha activity; shifts autonomic balance toward
  parasympathetic. <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6137615/>
- **Russo, M.A. et al. (2017).** *The physiological effects of slow breathing in
  the healthy human.* Breathe 13(4):298–309. **DOI 10.1183/20734735.009817.**
  Slow breathing → improved HRV, baroreflex sensitivity, sympathovagal balance.
  <https://publications.ersnet.org/content/breathe/13/4/298>
- **Brown, R.P. & Gerbarg, P.L. (2005).** *Sudarshan Kriya Yogic Breathing…
  Part I — Neurophysiologic Model.* J. Altern. Complement. Med. 11(1):189–201.
  **PMID 15750381.** Vagal/parasympathetic mechanism for calming. (Emerging —
  mechanistic model, not a trial.)

> **Note:** this effect does **not** depend on which nostril. It is AvirLog's
> most defensible evidence-based claim.

## 3 · Nostril side ↔ autonomic state — Emerging / contested

- **Werntz, D.A. et al. (1983).** *Alternating cerebral hemispheric activity and
  the lateralization of autonomic nervous function.* Hum. Neurobiol. 2(1):39–43.
  **PMID 6874437.** Foundational claim linking nostril dominance to hemispheric
  / autonomic lateralization. (Preliminary; not robustly replicated.)
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6874437/>
- **Telles, S. et al. (1994).** *Breathing through a particular nostril can alter
  metabolism and autonomic activities.* Indian J. Physiol. Pharmacol.
  38(2):133–137. **PMID 8063359.** Right-nostril practice raised O₂ consumption
  ~37% (sympathetic-like); left reduced sympathetic sweat activity.
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8063359/>
- **Pal, G.K. et al. (2014).** *Slow yogic breathing through right and left
  nostril influences sympathovagal balance…* N. Am. J. Med. Sci. 6(3):145–151.
  **PMID 24741554.** Left-nostril → parasympathetic/HRV gains; right → sympathetic.
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24741554/>
- **Larson, G. et al. (1993).** *Forced unilateral nostril breathing (FUNB)
  effects on the autonomic nervous system: an unsupported claim.* Med.
  Hypotheses 41(4):367–369. **PMID 8289706.** Controlled test found **no**
  significant changes in HR, pulse amplitude, temperature, skin conductance, or
  respiration. **Direct null result — cite for honesty.**
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8289706/>

## 4 · Effects on cognition / mood — Preliminary

- **Shannahoff-Khalsa, D.S. et al. (1991).** *The effects of unilateral forced
  nostril breathing on cognition.* Int. J. Neurosci. 57(3–4):239–249.
  **PMID 1938166.**
- **Jella, S.A. & Shannahoff-Khalsa, D.S. (1993).** *The effects of unilateral
  forced nostril breathing on cognitive performance.* Int. J. Neurosci.
  73(1–2):61–68. **PMID 8132419.** Spatial task better during left-nostril
  breathing (p=.028); verbal higher during right but non-significant (p=.14).
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8132419/>

## 5 · Traditional context — Traditional (heritage, not empirical)

- **Shiva Svarodaya (Shiva Swarodaya).** Sanskrit tantric text (~395 sutras), a
  dialogue of Shiva and Parvati; systematizes *swara yoga* — breath flowing
  dominantly through **ida** (left, cooling/lunar), **pingala** (right,
  warming/solar), or **sushumna** (central). Prescribes actions by dominance.
  Interpretive framework, labeled as heritage — never as measured fact.
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Swarodaya>

## How this maps to the app

| App surface | Backed by |
| --- | --- |
| "The nasal cycle is real" (Evidence · Established) | §1 |
| Slow-breathing / calm framing (Did-you-know, calm suggestions) | §2 |
| Right = active / left = calming **suggestions** (Log result card) | §3 — labeled *suggestion*, tested in Experiments |
| Focus/mood correlations (Experiments hypotheses) | §4 — explicitly n-of-1 |
| Ida / Pingala / Sushumna names + recommendations | §5 — labeled tradition |

**Guardrail:** anything from §3–§5 is presented as *hypothesis / suggestion /
heritage*, never as established fact. The null result (Larson 1993) is cited on
the very card that makes the right/left autonomic claim. That is the difference
between "confirming a belief" and "running a test."
