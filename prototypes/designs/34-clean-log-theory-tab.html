THEORY · WHY THIS APP EXISTS

The theory

Your nose keeps a real rhythm — airflow alternates between nostrils. Tradition read it first; science can now measure it. What each side means is still partly a hypothesis — which is what you get to test.
What tradition says
Ida left · mooncooling · rest · reflect
Pingala right · sunheating · focus · effort
Sushumna bothbalance · stillness
What science knows
Established

The nasal cycle is real

Airflow alternates between nostrils under autonomic control — seen in ~72% of people, over hours (mean ~2.6 h). Documented since 1895.

Kayser 1895 · Hasegawa & Kern 1977 · Eccles 1996
Established

Slow breathing calms you

Slow, paced breathing raises heart-rate variability and vagal tone — regardless of nostril. The app's firmest ground.

Zaccaro 2018 · Russo 2017 (reviews)
Emerging / contested

Right = active, left = calm?

Small studies point this way; one controlled study found no effect. A real question, unsettled — so the app treats it as a suggestion to test, not a fact.

Werntz 1983 · Telles 1994 · Larson 1993 (null)
Did you know

Chronobiology agrees the day isn't flat. Alertness rises and falls in ~90-minute waves (Kleitman's rest-activity cycle) — tradition told you to work with those waves long before we could chart them.
